## 📘 About This Project
Four-wheel independent steering and driving (4WISD) robots are widely used because of their high maneuverability. However, the strong coupling between actuator states and robot motion makes trajectory planning particularly challenging. Conventional methods often fail to fully exploit the motion capabilities of 4WISD robots, or generate trajectories with infeasible or energy-intensive motor actions. 

To address these issues, we propose a novel motor-state-oriented trajectory planning framework with motion-efficiency optimization. In the trajectory generation stage, motion primitives are generated directly from discretized motor inputs, thereby inherently satisfying motor performance limits. In the trajectory evaluation stage, motor energy consumption and whole-body obstacle evaluation are incorporated into a motion-efficiency objective that jointly considers safety, actuator feasibility, and energy efficiency. In the optimization stage, a time-decoupled strategy is introduced to handle complex constraints in real time while satisfying full-horizon motor-state constraints.

Simulation and real-world experiments on a self-built 4WISD platform demonstrate that the proposed framework enables safe, feasible, and efficient trajectory planning in unknown cluttered environments.

The source code will be released upon the acceptance of the paper.
